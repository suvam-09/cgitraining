
Question: What's the practical difference between 'ongoing monitoring' and 'effective challenge' - aren't they both just watching for problems?

Answer the above question with a real-life scenario (similar to the design-choice activity): Perceiving vs Reacting.


Answer:
Ongoing monitoring = Perceiving
Effective challenge = Reacting (i.e., questioning what we perceive)

Real-life scenario: Imagine a team designing an office building. They have chosen large glass windows because the design brief says the building should have lots of space for natural light.

During the design process:
Ongoing monitoring — “What we see?”
The project team keeps checking whether the design is performing as expected.
Now, consider they notice from energy simulations that the large glass façade is causing significant heat gain in the afternoon.
This is monitoring: identifying a signal, trend, deviation, or emerging problem.

Effective challenge — Based on the perceived scenario, “So what? Should we accept this?”
A reviewer doesn't simply record the result. They challenge the design choice:
“Why are we continuing with this amount of glazing if it's creating a substantial cooling penalty? Have we tested external shading or a different glazing specification? Are we prioritising the original aesthetic choice over the energy-performance requirement?”
Now, it's an effective challenge: questioning assumptions, decisions, evidence, and proposed responses—and pushing for action when warranted.
