Ongoing Monitoring vs Effective Challenge

What is the practical difference between ongoing monitoring and effective challenge? Aren't they both just watching for problems?

The Practical Difference

Both activities involve paying attention to what is happening, but they play different roles in the decision-making process.

Ongoing monitoring = Perceiving

Effective challenge = Reacting
i.e., questioning what we perceive

Real-Life Scenario: Designing an Office Building

Imagine a team designing an office building. They have chosen large glass windows because the design brief says the building should have lots of space for natural light.

1
Perceiving

Ongoing Monitoring — "What do we see?"

During the design process, the project team keeps checking whether the design is performing as expected.

Now, consider that they notice from energy simulations that the large glass façade is causing significant heat gain in the afternoon.

"The simulations show that afternoon cooling demand is much higher than expected."

This is monitoring: identifying a signal, trend, deviation, or emerging problem.

2
Reacting

Effective Challenge — "So what? Should we accept this?"

Based on the perceived scenario, a reviewer doesn't simply record the result. They challenge the design choice.

"Why are we continuing with this amount of glazing if it's creating a substantial cooling penalty? Have we tested external shading or a different glazing specification? Are we prioritising the original aesthetic choice over the energy-performance requirement?"

Now, it's an effective challenge: questioning assumptions, decisions, evidence, and proposed responses — and pushing for action when warranted.

Perceiving vs Reacting

🔵 Ongoing Monitoring

Perceiving what is happening.

  • Observe
  • Measure
  • Track
  • Detect signals
  • Identify emerging problems

🟣 Effective Challenge

Reacting to what has been perceived.

  • Question
  • Probe
  • Test assumptions
  • Evaluate evidence
  • Drive appropriate action

Key Takeaway

Ongoing monitoring perceives. It tells us what is happening and highlights signals, trends or emerging problems.


Effective challenge reacts. It asks what those signals mean, questions whether the current decision is still appropriate, and helps drive the right action.


In simple terms: Monitoring detects the signal. Effective challenge decides what to do about it.
